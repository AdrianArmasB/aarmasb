Research

I work on macroeconomics, international finance, and trade, with an emphasis on emerging economies — dollarization, exchange-rate policy, and the macroeconomics of fertility and long-run growth.

Working Papers

Dollarization Hysteresis and Expectation Formation In progress

Third-year paper · 2025

Abstract
Deposit dollarization persists in many emerging economies despite decades of inflation stability. I propose that heterogeneous beliefs about inflation and currency depreciation — formed through experience-based learning (EBL) — explain why older cohorts maintain higher foreign-currency deposits than younger ones. Using an overlapping-generations model with endogenous currency-portfolio choice and EBL-based expectation formation, I show how past experiences, belief updating, and demography jointly shape the persistence of aggregate dollarization. The reduced-form evidence uses household financial-survey data from Uruguay to quantify how past inflation experiences determine current deposit dollarization across cohorts.

Fertility Free-Riding and Optimal Transfers

with Brian C. Fujiy · 2025

Abstract
U.S. fertility has declined for decades, threatening long-run economic growth. This paper identifies a fertility free-riding problem: additional births generate positive externalities for society, but individual families bear all the costs and do not capture the full social value. We embed a semi-endogenous growth model into an overlapping-generations framework with endogenous fertility, capturing both intensive (number of children) and extensive (share of families with children) margins, and design an optimal transfer system. Using American Community Survey data, we analyze whether fertility is inefficiently low and quantify the effects of optimal transfers on U.S. fertility rates and long-run growth.

Exchange Rate Determination and Optimal FXI Policy: The Role of Portfolio and Liquidity Shocks

with Marco Ortiz · 2022 · SBS Peru Working Paper Series

Abstract
A growing number of central banks intervene in foreign-exchange markets while operating under inflation targeting. This paper develops a framework to guide their instrument choice. We model incomplete and segmented financial markets in which financial intermediaries price FX costs and liquidity trade-offs, and we use the framework to analyze optimal FXI policy using spot and derivative instruments.

Presented at: LACEA-LAMES Annual Meeting (2022) · Money, Macro and Finance Society Annual Meeting (2022) · INFER Annual Meeting (2022) · Banco Central de Reserva del Perú Annual Meeting and Seminar (2022) · Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú Seminar (2022)

Earlier Work

Default on Debt Payment and Loss of Formal Employment in Peru

SBS Peru Working Paper Series

Abstract
Quantifies the effect of losing formal employment on debt repayment in Peru. I find that losing a formal job increases past-due days by 8.7 per quarter on average, with heterogeneous effects by income and age.

Liquidity Management in a Bi-Monetary Banking System

CEMFI M.Sc. thesis (advisor: Nezih Guner) · SBS Peru Working Paper Series

Abstract
A banking model for a bi-monetary system with local and foreign currency. Banks access exchange-rate, FX-swap, and interbank markets. I analyze U.S. monetary-policy spillovers and FX-swap market dynamics.

Presented at the Asociación Peruana de Economía Annual Meeting (2019) and the Banco Central de Reserva del Perú Annual Meeting (2019).

Liquidity Management in a Totally Dollarized Economy

Universidad del Pacífico M.Sc. thesis (advisor: Saki Bigio) · SBS Peru Working Paper Series

Abstract
A banking model for a fully dollarized economy without a lender of last resort. I show that some parameter configurations generate a narrow-banking strategy in equilibrium.
